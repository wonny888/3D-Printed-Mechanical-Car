# Mechanical Car Design — "Blue"

An electrically powered vehicle designed, fabricated, and tested as part of an engineering design project. The car combines CAD-based mechanical design, laser-cut fabrication, gear transmission design, electrical integration, and iterative performance testing within a compact blue-whale-inspired chassis.

## Project Overview

The objective of this project was to design and construct a motor-driven vehicle capable of traveling at least **10 feet autonomously** while meeting strict size, material, and mechanical constraints.

The final vehicle, named **Blue**, was designed around a compact four-wheel architecture with a laser-cut plywood chassis and a single-stage gear transmission. Mechanical performance was balanced with a blue-whale-inspired exterior featuring side flippers, a rear tail fin, and a functional blowhole switch integrated into the electrical system.

The design process involved CAD modeling, drivetrain selection, fabrication, electrical assembly, and repeated physical testing. Early drivetrain configurations experienced gear grinding and poor meshing, requiring adjustments to gear selection, shaft alignment, and component spacing before reaching the final configuration.

## Key Features

- **Custom Drivetrain:** 11-tooth motor pinion driving a 45-tooth output gear for a **4.1:1 reduction ratio**, balancing wheel speed and startup torque.

- **CAD-Driven Design:** Complete vehicle geometry, drivetrain layout, structural components, assembly drawings, and mass properties developed before fabrication.

- **Laser-Cut Chassis:** Structural body fabricated from **1/8-inch plywood** with interlocking panels, axle supports, and removable access to internal components.

- **Electrical Integration:** DC motor, battery system, wiring, and a top-mounted **blowhole switch** integrated into the mechanical structure.

- **Iterative Testing:** Gear alignment, wheel positioning, chassis geometry, and structural attachments were refined through multiple prototypes to eliminate drivetrain grinding and improve stability.

## Mechanical Design

The vehicle uses a rear-wheel-drive configuration powered by the supplied DC motor. An **11-tooth pinion gear** meshes with a **45-tooth output gear**, producing a gear ratio of approximately **4.1:1**.

The rear output shaft is mechanically coupled to the driven wheels while the front axle remains free-rolling to reduce resistance. Metal rod axles and careful axle alignment were used to minimize friction and improve directional stability.

The battery pack and motor were positioned low within the chassis to maintain a stable center of mass. The whale-themed exterior was designed around the drivetrain so that decorative components would not interfere with wheel rotation or transmission operation.

## Design & Fabrication

- **CAD Modeling:** SolidWorks
- **Structural Material:** 1/8-inch laser-cut plywood
- **Power Source:** DC electric motor
- **Transmission:** Spur gear drivetrain
- **Fabrication:** Laser cutting, mechanical assembly, soldering, and wiring
- **Mechanical Components:** Metal rod axles, kit wheels, gears, and custom structural panels

## Performance

The final vehicle successfully completed the required **10-foot autonomous run in approximately 1.8 seconds**.

- **Distance:** 10 ft
- **Run Time:** ~1.8 s
- **Final Mass:** 162.8 g
- **Gear Ratio:** 4.1:1
- **Drivetrain:** Rear-wheel drive

The final drivetrain operated without the gear grinding observed in earlier prototypes. A slight rightward drift remained due to chassis/axle alignment, but the vehicle maintained stability throughout the run.

## Engineering Iteration

Early transmission designs used more aggressive gear combinations that produced excessive grinding, vibration, and inconsistent motion. Through testing, the drivetrain geometry and gear selection were modified to improve gear meshing and power transmission.

The final 4.1:1 configuration provided smoother operation and increased vehicle speed. Additional testing identified wheel alignment and structural attachment as important factors affecting directional stability and reliability.

The project demonstrated the importance of **gear-ratio selection, mechanical alignment, friction reduction, rapid prototyping, and design iteration** in small-scale vehicle development.
